The official South Korea Digital Nomad Visa (officially known as the Workation Visa / F-1-D) allows foreign remote workers and digital nomads from Europe and worldwide to legally live in South Korea for up to 2 years while working for overseas companies.

Before this landmark visa initiative, travelers from European nations (such as the UK, Germany, France, Italy, and the Netherlands) were limited to a 90-day visa-free tourist stay. Now, remote employees and high-earning freelancers can establish their base in dynamic neighborhoods like Hongdae, Gangnam, or Busan while enjoying world-class internet speed, rich cuisine, and safe 24/7 city infrastructure.

In this comprehensive guide updated for 2026, we break down the annual income requirements, required embassy documents, Alien Registration Card (ARC) process, and step-by-step application protocol for European passport holders.

1. Korea Digital Nomad Visa (F-1-D) Overview & Key Benefits

The F-1-D visa is tailor-made for high-income remote professionals who wish to immerse themselves in Korean culture without paying local income taxes on foreign-sourced earnings.

Category Details & Regulation
Initial Stay Duration 1 Year from the date of entry (Multiple Entry)
Extension Option Extendable for 1 additional year at local immigration (Total 2 Years)
Dependent Accompanying Spouses and minor children under 18 can accompany with dependent F-1-D status
Local Employment Restriction Strictly prohibited from working for Korean companies or taking domestic jobs

2. Eligibility Criteria & Minimum Income Requirement

To qualify for the Korean Digital Nomad Visa, applicants must meet strict financial, employment, and medical insurance criteria:

  1. Minimum Age: Must be at least 18 years old.
  2. Minimum Annual Income (GNI Threshold ⭐):
    • Applicants must earn more than double the previous year’s Korean Gross National Income (GNI) per capita.
    • For 2026, this threshold is approximately 88 million to 90 million KRW (approx. €60,000 to €63,000 EUR / £52,000 GBP annually) before taxes.
    • Income must originate entirely from overseas employers or overseas business clients.
  3. Work Experience: Must have worked in the same industry/field as a remote employee or freelancer for at least 1 year.
  4. Comprehensive Private Health Insurance: Must possess international health insurance covering medical evacuation and hospital treatment of at least 100 million KRW (approx. €70,000 EUR) in South Korea.
  5. Clean Criminal Background: An apostilled criminal record check certificate issued by your home country's government.

3. Required Documents Checklist for European Applicants

Ensure all documents are translated into English or Korean and officially apostilled where required:

  • ✅ Valid European Passport with at least 6 months remaining validity.
  • ✅ Completed Visa Application Form with a passport-sized color photograph (3.5cm x 4.5cm).
  • ✅ Certificate of Employment / Business License verifying remote status.
  • ✅ Bank Statements & Payslips (Past 12 Months) demonstrating regular income meeting the GNI threshold.
  • ✅ Apostilled Criminal Background Check (e.g., DBS Certificate in the UK, Führungszeugnis in Germany, Casier Judiciaire in France).
  • ✅ International Medical Insurance Certificate showing minimum 100M KRW emergency coverage.
  • ✅ Visa Application Fee (Varies by embassy, typically €60 to €90 EUR).

4. Step-by-Step Application Protocol

  1. Step 1: Document Preparation & Apostille Gather your 12-month payslips, bank statements, remote work verification letter from your employer, and get your police background check apostilled.
  2. Step 2: Submit to Korean Embassy/Consulate in Europe Book an appointment at the Embassy of the Republic of Korea in your home country (London, Berlin, Paris, Rome, Madrid, etc.) and submit the physical dossier. (If you are already in Korea on a B-2/K-ETA visa, check with your local immigration office for status change eligibility).
  3. Step 3: Visa Approval & Entry Processing typically takes between 2 to 4 weeks. Upon approval, you will receive your visa grant notice online.
  4. Step 4: Register for ARC (Alien Registration Card) in Seoul Within 90 days of arriving in Korea, book an appointment at your jurisdiction's immigration office (e.g., Seoul Immigration Office in Mokdong or Sejongno) to obtain your physical ARC card. The ARC enables you to sign high-speed home internet contracts, open local bank accounts, and register Korean phone numbers.

5. Best Coworking & Living Hubs in Seoul for European Expats

  • Hongdae / Yeonnam-dong (Mapo-gu): The creative heart of Seoul. Packed with aesthetic cafes, open late into the night, vibrant international community, and walking distance to Han River.
  • Seongsu-dong (Seongdong-gu): The 'Brooklyn of Seoul'. Trendy renovated warehouse cafes, top tech startups, and boutique coworking spaces.
  • Gangnam / Teheran-ro: The corporate innovation corridor. Home to WeWork, FastFive, 24/7 facilities, and direct transit to all major metropolitan districts.

6. Summary & Final Tips

  • ✔ Double-GNI income rule (~€60k EUR) is strictly audited via tax certificates and bank records.
  • ✔ Medical insurance must have full hospitalization coverage in Korea.
  • ✔ Once granted, you enjoy 1 to 2 years of seamless, tax-efficient remote working in the world's most hyper-connected metropolis.
